Key takeaways

Six things the data tells us about how Tovertafel is used. Note: data collection goes back to 2023, with usage growing exponentially in recent years. Most events in this dataset are from the past 1 to 2 years.
1

Summer is underrepresented, and that is expected

Spring has the most plays (125,647) and Winter follows closely with 113,397. Summer comes in very low at just 11,551, but this simply reflects where we are in the year. The data runs up to spring 2026 and summer has not started yet, so those months are still missing. As the dataset grows, summer figures will fill in.

2

People play most in the evening

The busiest hour globally is 23:00 local time with 15,115 plays, and activity stays high from 16:00 right through to midnight. This points strongly to Tovertafel being part of regular evening care routines, likely used as a way to keep residents engaged and help them wind down before sleep.

3

Germany plays completely different games from everyone else

BEACHBALL and LEAVES top the charts in almost every country. Germany is the clear exception. Its top five games are GARDENING, FIREFLIES, KITES, SPINNINGTOPS and DINNER PARTY, none of which feature in the global top ten. The Netherlands also stands out, with BIRTHDAY ranked first. These differences likely come down to local care staff choices and cultural context.

4

How long people play matters as much as how often they start

The typical session lasts just 5.2 minutes, which fits the drop-in style of care home use. Even so, PUZZLE has built up around 1,150 total hours of play, more than any other game, because titles like PUZZLE UGC and SNOEZEL CLOUDS hold attention for 14 to 15 minutes at a time. Raw launch counts do not tell the full story.

5

Most devices drop off within the first month

Around 80 devices were still active at day 5 after first use. By day 30 only about a third of those were still active. After that the curve levels off, and a small group of committed devices keeps playing well past 300 days. The early drop-off is likely a mix of trial periods at new care facilities and the typical onboarding curve for any new technology.

6

The US leads in volume, but Europe shows far more variety

The US accounts for roughly half of all plays, with Australia and Canada close behind. European countries have lower totals but show much more variety in which games they prefer. This suggests that local care philosophy and staff choices shape game selection more than anything else, and that there is real room to tailor recommendations by country.
